# Japan - The Kitayama Concord

Version 0.3.0-alpha.78 for Europa Universalis V 1.3.

This is an external testing alpha. It is feature-rich and playable, but its
balance, AI behavior, compatibility, interface polish, and long-campaign
stability still need wider testing.

## AI Japan and House campaigns

Alpha.75 enables AI administration and project actions. A clan or House player
activates the temporary House-privilege direction described in
[AI Japan support](AI_JAPAN_SUPPORT.md). Runtime campaign testing is pending. See [OBSERVER_TESTS.md](OBSERVER_TESTS.md) for the two release-candidate runs.

## Premise

The Kitayama Concord is an alternate path for Japan that avoids the Sengoku
Jidai by redirecting the warrior houses toward commerce, infrastructure, and
negotiated government. The old clans become Chartered Buke Houses within a
permanent economic compact. They elect the shogun, vote on national laws,
compete through buildings and credit, fund a common Treasury, and gradually
entrench a new constitutional order.

## Major features

- A new Kitayama Concord international organization replacing the Japanese
  Shogunate without annexing the player into an elected House
- Approximately 140 Chartered Houses, protected from internal war while the
  compact survives
- Economically weighted shogunal elections, a three-House founding senior
  council, and additional Senior Charter seats unlocked every 10 Entrenchment
- Concord Cohesion and century-long Concord Entrenchment progress bars
- Eight families of three-option laws covering government, markets, arms,
  roads, arbitration, assessments, maritime policy, and credit, with distinct
  economic and administrative roles rather than repeated generic bonuses;
  middle and ambitious laws impose increasing recurring Cohesion costs
- A separate House War Service law determining whether Japan cannot call
  Houses, may invite them voluntarily, or automatically mobilizes every House
- Prototype Concord Commercial Protectorate and Chartered Extraction
  Commission subject constitutions, plus four licensed overseas House
  concessions for trade, resources, sailors, and manpower
- Six recurring overseas-subject consequences covering harbor petitions,
  mixed legal jurisdiction, extraction arrears, charter reform, competing
  House claims, and Treasury remittances. Extraction Commissions may be
  reformed into Commercial Protectorates or tightened at a Cohesion cost;
  their uncapped Treasury values scale from the selected subject's economy
- A Southern Islands Survey opening thirty years of Taiwanese settlement and
  Ryukyuan diplomacy; province-by-province Taiwanese registers; five-income-
  year loyalty compacts with Tondo, Cebu, and Butuan that transfer local
  settlement rights; and a Northern Colonial Administration that absorbs
  later Japanese settlements in upper Manchuria and Sakhalin
- Four Red-Seal Charter events beginning in 1590 and an adaptive 1609 Ryukyu
  Settlement for independent, subject, or directly administered islands
- The late-century Continental War That Never Was: diplomatic and commercial
  conclusions or a one-war Korean settlement followed by direct rule, one
  Commercial Protectorate, or four Chartered Extraction Commissions
- Two reusable treaty-coast doctrines against the current `CHI` mandate
  holder. Each eligible Chinese coastal area costs exactly 20 war score and
  becomes either a Commercial Protectorate or an Extraction Commission rather
  than Japanese core territory
- Inheritable overseas concession licenses: ordinary Houses begin with one,
  Senior Houses receive a second, larger Treasury subscriptions buy larger
  license packages, and both installed concessions and unused licenses pass
  to a House purchaser
- A four-event 1582 Great Embassy chain reframing the Tensho mission as a
  contest among the Secretariat, maritime Houses, court diplomacy, and
  Christian scholarship
- A paid Concord Secretariat bureaucracy: low Cohesion naturally recovers,
  while sustained high Cohesion requires an adjustable recurring state
  expense; funding also reduces Diplomatic Spending costs, its permanent
  records modestly improve Estate Satisfaction Recovery, and its funded
  effectiveness grows with native bureaucracy Entrenchment; at full funding,
  25-50% of its nominal upkeep is remitted into the common Treasury
- Intended Cohesion equilibria are 80-100 for accommodating governments,
  60-80 for mixed governments, and 40-60 for fully ambitious governments.
- A Japanese/Shogunate-keyed visual suite for the Concord organization,
  member ranks, unique buildings and advances, government systems, transition
  crises, collapse, and the Hundred-Year Concord.
- A second-century Phase 2 foundation after the Hundred-Year Articles: a
  three-way opening precedent, six unique Renaissance advancements, the paid
  Office of Maritime Exchange bureaucracy, and mutually exclusive Councils of
  Licensed Ports or National Board of Exchange reforms
- The Tallies of Ningbo, a four-event 1523 maritime dispute that turns the
  historical clash of rival missions into common registry, Chartered House,
  or court-guaranteed oceanic law and automatically grants Licensed Oceanic
  Convoys
- The Southern Sea Exchange, a multi-year Situation covering matchlock
  standardization, powder supply, silver clearing, translation, a
  religion-neutral foreign-worship precedent, and one selected Southern
  Exchange Hall; successful ratification grants the firearms, silver, and
  translation advancements automatically
- A later Colonial Survey Registers chain that begins once Japan has completed
  the Southern Sea Exchange, controls a colonial nation, and knows a country
  whose capital lies in Europe; the same conditions give Kyoto one monthly New
  World Institution progress until it adopts the institution
- A common Treasury with contributions, mediation, annual House assessments,
  arrears, emergency programs, credit stabilization, public works, common
  granaries, security appropriations, and maritime underwriting
- Six permanent, five-stage Treasury great works whose costs double with each
  upgrade: Grand Irrigation Basins, a National Kaido Spine, an Inland Sea
  Harbor Chain, National Floodworks, Transmontane Tunnels, and National Urban
  Waterworks. They share a progressively revealed National Great Projects view
  under the Concord's Administration tab. Irrigation, Harbor, Floodworks, and
  Waterworks stages unlock five local building levels which Japan must construct,
  staff, and maintain; later stages require a functioning national network.
  Kaido and Tunnels remain route-wide projects. The latter three works unlock
  naturally through the Second National Works Register after the Southern Sea
  Exchange, and new towns must construct their own water system.
- A separate 10,000-gold Metropolitan Charter that transforms one qualifying
  400,000-population urban location into the Concord's unique Megalopolis
- Arms and maritime laws that now govern the AI's intended House force size
  and maintenance support while preserving recruitment from owned clan
  military buildings and shipbuilding from owned Clan Shipyards
- Compulsory Charter Redemption, allowing wealthy Houses to acquire weaker
  Houses while preserving a minimum competitive membership
- Unique House, state, infrastructure, fiscal, agricultural, industrial, and
  Ainu commercial buildings
- Entrenchment milestones, early Banking progress, unique advancements,
  Professional Armies opportunities, and a Renaissance reward
- An Ezo Compact with protected Ainu communities, settlement policies,
  restored tribesmen populations, and northern commercial competition
- Twelve recurring political-economic Concord events with shared and
  story-specific cooldowns, covering credit, roads, retainers, religious
  trusts, village petitions, customs, food reserves, and council precedence
- Ten additional Phase 2 events covering firearms patents, powder leakage,
  silver clearing, treaty translation, port charities, temple schools,
  colonial boundaries, concealed revenue, slave trading, and returning pilots
- A compact Christian-contact chain centered on registered relief and civil
  status, with an explicitly extreme player-only path to convert Japan and the
  ruling family to Catholicism
- Five linked foundational event chains spanning 1401-1466: the Chinese Tally
  Trade, the Concord after Yoshimitsu, Tsushima and the wakō, the Shōchō Debt
  Crisis, and the early constitutional government of Japan's port towns
- The War That Never Was Situation, redirecting the pressures of the Ōnin War
  into an electoral deadlock, credit freeze, warehouse seizures, rival patrols,
  withheld trade tallies, and three constitutional settlements
- Northern discoveries for Shimamaki iron sand, Niikappu horse breeding, and
  a rare alternate-history early discovery of the Itomuka mercury deposit
- Eight post-capstone Concord estate privileges: paired House Privilege and
  Concord Commonwealth settlements for Nobles, Burghers, Clergy, and Peasants
- The Unquiet Peace crisis at 30 Entrenchment and The Great Reckoning at 70
- A multi-year Hundred-Year Concord Situation at 100 Entrenchment, with three
  constitutional readings, a Printing Press reward, publication legacies, two
  mutually exclusive Age 1 reforms, and the unique House Privilege vs Concord
  Commonwealth societal value
- Merchants of Competing Europes, a four-event chain that turns Portuguese,
  Spanish, Dutch, English, and other company rivalry into common auctions,
  House brokerage, or national commercial registration without binding Japan
  to one European patron
- Japanese Overseas Communities, a five-event chain covering Nihonmachi,
  mixed courts, families of two shores, returning merchants, and permanent
  Registered Nihonmachi modifiers in qualifying overseas subject capitals
- Regulation of the Open Seas, a 1630s Situation with six ratified articles,
  three visible constitutional influence tracks, dedicated artwork, and three
  final reforms replacing the provisional Council or National Board
- The Fracturing Concord disaster at low Cohesion, with emergency recovery
  tools and a full collapse into a Warring Houses Interregnum

## How to found the Concord

1. Begin as the leader of the Japanese Shogunate.
2. Resolve the Nanboku-cho situation.
3. Be at peace and reach at least 25 Stability (15 for AI-controlled Japan).
4. Move Aristocracy vs Plutocracy to 0 or farther toward Plutocracy.
5. Open the Japanese Shogunate, select Members, and use
   **Proclaim the Kitayama Concord**.
6. Open the new Concord with its native bottom-screen organization button.
7. Use **Ratify the Kitayama Concord**. This safely removes the old
   Shogunate, and the Ratify action then disappears permanently.

## Installing from this repository

Download or clone the repository. Create a folder named `Japan - The Kitayama Concord`
inside your EU5 user `mod` directory, then copy `.metadata`, `in_game`,
`main_menu`, `thumbnail.png`, and the included documentation into it. Keep
`.metadata` intact: it identifies the mod to the game. Enable the mod in your
playset and disable duplicate Workshop/local copies. A fresh campaign is
recommended; saves made without this mod are unsupported.

This repository starts with **0.3.0-alpha.78**. AI formation at 15 Stability
has been confirmed in an observer run. The northern colonization changes
passed static tests and still require in-game observer validation.

See [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md) for the repository layout and release notes.

## Installing the unlisted alpha

1. Open the unlisted Steam Workshop link supplied in the Discord post.
2. Subscribe to the mod and allow Steam to finish downloading it.
3. Enable **Japan - The Kitayama Concord (Alpha)** in an EU5 playset.
4. Disable other gameplay mods for the first test whenever possible.
5. Start a fresh campaign. Saves created without this mod are unsupported.

## Known alpha issues

- English localization only
- Balance and AI House behavior remain provisional
- The three founding Senior Charters represent the Hosokawa, Shiba, and
  Hatakeyama Kanrei lineages; EU5 labels far more clans as shugo daimyo than
  the new three-seat starting capacity can support
- EU5 logs validation warnings for unrestricted foreign House buildings; the
  lack of a relations gate is intentional
- Non-producing House buildings produce a one-time no-production-method
  warning because dummy upkeep methods were deliberately removed
- One harmless election message-type warning may appear
- Some native IO and construction tooltips can emit null-context GUI warnings
  without a visible gameplay failure
- Compatibility with mods changing Japan, the Japanese Shogunate, Japanese
  clan buildings, the Middle Kingdom, international organizations, or the same
  GUI panels is not expected in this alpha
- The Concord replaces the full vanilla Middle Kingdom definition solely to
  add its House join restriction; this file must be reviewed after EU5 updates

## Northern colonization

AI Japan pursues Ezo settlement after Concord formation and upper Manchuria/Sakhalin after completing the Hundred-Year Concord. Dedicated paid charter actions work under the western-only AI colonization rule. The AI receives basic colonization technology and northern charts at the relevant stage; native range, charter costs and settlement progress still apply. Players have no Entrenchment restriction on colonization. The Northern Colonial Administration can be chartered from the first northern holding after Concord formation.

Compatibility: this version also replaces the native create_colonial_charter and abandon_colonial_charter action definitions to route Concord AI northern settlement through the dedicated actions and retain its active northern charters. Other countries and human controls retain vanilla behavior. Mods replacing those actions require a compatibility patch.
